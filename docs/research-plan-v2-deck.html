Means of Prediction · research plan v2 · September 17, 2026

We proved an AI can read the email.
Now we find out if a market can settle on it.

The last two weeks tuned prompts on 161 examples. That answered the easy question. This plan goes after the three things that actually decide the product: is the email real news, does it arrive in time, and can a cheater fool the system.

Where we stand

So

Reading is solved if you can trust the reader. The chain can only trust a small one. And most newsletters arrive too late. Better prompts fix none of that, which is why the prompt-tuning loop is stopped.

Two wake-up calls from independent reviewers

"Signed" is not "written by the newsroom", and cheaters get many tries

1 · the support-desk trick

The email signature proves the newspaper's domain sent the message. It does not prove a journalist wrote it. Email a paper's help desk the words "BREAKING: X convicted", and the automatic reply quotes your words back, signed by the paper. The key list we built includes help-desk keys, staff mail keys and weak old keys. Markets must name the exact newsletter senders they accept, and require at least two different papers.

The question now

Four unknowns. Everything we do must move one of them.

1 · real news?

Admissible evidence

Which senders are genuinely the newsroom's newsletters, and which fit what the contract can check?

2 · in time?

Coverage and timing

For which kinds of events does an email arrive, how fast, and is the fact in the subject line or buried in the body?

3 · cheat-proof?

Verifiable reading

Can a settlement that only trusts a small checkable AI match the big one, even against a cheater?

4 · clear rules?

Rule design

What must a market's rules say so an email report is unambiguous evidence?

Running now

First test: give the small AI only the passage the big AI found, change nothing else. Shows how much of the 41% → 93% gap pointing alone closes.

Next, and more important

The cheater test: slide every possible passage from the trick emails past the small AI and count how many markets end up with any wrong "YES".

Ship something early

Subject lines first

Why

Breaking-news alerts put the fact in the subject line and arrive within minutes. That is a headline-sized job the small checkable AI already handles, with no body text to publish and far lower cost.

By day 14

Three real test markets on the Sepolia test network, settled from subject lines, requiring two accepted papers each, with the AI judge running silently alongside the existing pattern-matching rules.

Decision rule, set in advance

If at least 70% of timely settlements in the live test come from subject lines, the product launches on subject lines and the point-then-decide design stays in research.

The honest test

Write the bets first. Then wait for the news.

The only test where the AI cannot already know the answer. All markets are written down and locked before the events happen.

Group A · 120 markets

Scheduled events

Votes, verdict dates, award nights, finals, rate decisions, one to four weeks ahead. The easy case.

Group B · 120 markets

Surprises

"Will X happen by date Y" questions picked at random from open markets. The realistic case.

Group C · traps

Look-alikes

For every event, two near-miss markets (wrong person, wrong office, wrong date) so half of all markets should stay "NO".

Reported per event type: did an email arrive within 48 hours, how long settlement took, correct, wrong, and unsettled.
