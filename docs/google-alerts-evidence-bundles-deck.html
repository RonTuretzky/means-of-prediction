Problem definition

A signed alert is the beginning of the proof.

01 · AUTHENTICITY

Did Google sign these bytes?

Verify the signature and complete body hash; retain the original.
02 · COMPLETENESS

Is every required fact present?

Missing dates, qualifiers or a results table can prevent a decision.
03 · ADMISSIBILITY

Does this market accept it?

Source, event instance, finality and exception clauses still apply.
04 · EXECUTION

Can the resolver verify it?

Implement the policy with bounded cost; reject unsupported outcomes.

Complementary queries mainly improve discovery and completeness. They cannot manufacture an official result, establish exhaustive coverage or change a market’s source policy.

Keep separate statuses: authenticated → relevant → rule-complete → executed. Require evidence for every transition.
DKIM specificationGoogle Alerts helpAdmissibility and execution distinctions derive from saved rules and source review.

Rule-first design

Before creating an alert, write the acceptance checklist.

Identity: entity, event, year, round/session and jurisdiction.
Proposition: completed action, winner, number or exact availability condition.
Time: occurrence, publication, cutoff and timezone.
Authority: named source, fallback and independence standard.
Finality: first announcement, classification, revisions and cancellation.

A small rule record

event: Singapore / 2026 / Grand Prix race
predicate: official final position == 1
entity: named driver
source: exact market source clause
version: rule-defined final race classification
exceptions: postponement / cancellation
unknown: any required fact absent

Schema sketch. Preserve the literal market text, a rule hash and review of ambiguous clauses.

Generate queries from missing requirements. The desired answer inside a query is never evidence.

Market drill-down · conditions behind the headline

“Announced” often leaves the decisive condition unproved.

Product availability

For the mapped free-access market: exact product, official channel, actual public availability, nonpaid eligibility and launch time.

A free trial of a paid plan or closed beta does not qualify. Usage-limited free access can.

site:[official host] "[exact product]" "available" "free plan"

Join release notes and eligibility documentation only for the same version and rollout.

Commercial or formal action

A licensing market needs a binding grant of the specified commercial rights. Evaluation or development collaboration can be insufficient.

An election-call market may need formal scheduling under that jurisdiction, beyond political intent.

"[party A]" "[party B]" "license agreement" site:[official host]

Seek the missing operative act, date and scope. Repetition does not supply them.

When a clause requires interpretation the deterministic policy cannot express, retain “needs review.” Do not let a model quietly fill the gap.
Saved product-availability, licensing and Spain-election rules. Examples describe rule conditions, not current product or legal status.
Cryptographic construction

Combine verified claims; preserve the signed envelopes.

Verify every original

email A → RSA + full-body hash → receipt A
email B → RSA + full-body hash → receipt B
email C → RSA + full-body hash → receipt C

A signature over A does not authenticate A+B. Changing the canonicalized body, cropping or stitching breaks its signed body-hash binding.

Evaluate the bundle separately

receipt A → event + completed result
receipt B → official status + finality
receipt C → permitted corroboration

fixed rule + bound evidence → decision

Each decisive field must come from signed bytes through verified code or an explicitly trusted attestation.

Authenticated A ∧ authenticated B
+ valid join + complete rule ⇒ admissible bundle
A bundle hash or Merkle root commits to items; it does not verify their truth, signatures or extraction. DKIM’s flat body hash also gives no ordinary Merkle path for a cropped snippet.

Provenance and joins

Bind headline, snippet and source link in the same result block.

EvidenceItem {
  emailReceipt, resultIndex, signedByteRanges,
  targetURL, exactHost, storyLineage,
  eventKey, entity, predicate, value, unit,
  eventTime, publicationTime, signedEmailDate,
  observedDeliveryTime,
  sourceStatus, version, extractionProfile
}

Caller-supplied JSON beside a valid email is insufficient. Verify field extraction—or disclose who attested it.

Join guardrails

  • Same event, year, session and entity.
  • Compatible units, version and time basis.
  • Text belongs to the URL being credited.
  • No subject or query banner as evidence.
  • Missing clauses and conflicts stay visible.
  • Never label stitched snippets as a reconstructed original article.
Bind chain, market, rule hash, evidence IDs, parser version and outcome in the proof manifest. Reuse verified emails across eligible markets; deduplicate stories and facts inside each decision.

Operational and trust limits

Alerts provides a useful, incomplete view of the web.

Delivery and content

Indexing, selection, timing and truncation can hide decisive details. A linked page can change after capture. More queries do not establish exhaustive recall.

Keys and timestamps

Keep originals, hashes and key snapshots. Our registry trusts a registrar; a current key snapshot is not complete historical-validity proof. Signed email Date is not event time; observed inbox delivery is separate collection metadata.

Public news, private mail

Raw alerts can contain a recipient address and alert-management links or tokens. Publishing the whole body can expose these even when the news is public.

The market must explicitly accept its evidence class. Google DKIM authenticates Google’s relayed bytes; it does not certify the linked publisher’s truth or independent authorship.

Fetching a linked article can help a reviewer. For settlement, that content needs a separately defined, accepted authentication or attestation path.

Cost and size

Chunks can solve transport limits while costing more.

Current pagination

Stores one canonical body in immutable pages up to 24,000 bytes, then reconstructs it for full hash, RSA and predicate verification.

It does not combine messages or divide final verification work. Stored bytes are public; nine pages share the 192 KiB body limit.

Practical plan

Fix the evidence representation before multiplying the inbox.

Build obligation sheets and fix provenance. Exact event keys and authority hosts; same-block text/link binding; missing facts and lineage deduplication.
Run the query comparison in shadow mode. Begin with categorical winner/advancement events. Cap variants; retire those adding no useful evidence.
Build a narrow bundle resolver. Authenticate independent receipts; verify typed conjunctions; explicitly handle insufficient evidence in new markets.
Benchmark proof backends. Same real alert bodies and predicates through native receipt, pagination, ZK and attested extraction paths.
Expand where results support it. Add structured-source capture for tables and counts; keep unresolved semantic cases in review.
Admission rule: prefer an observable decisive fact, an obtainable accepted source, manageable finality and an expressible resolver. Screening out unsuitable markets is a useful product feature.
Recommended sequencing, not validated schedule/cost. Existing daily collection can continue unchanged while a separate experiment is prepared.

Decision

Build a system that knows what evidence is missing.

Use complementary alerts for specific rule requirements. Verify each email separately, then evaluate a provenance-bound bundle.

Most promising

Discrete completed events, short official results and categorical facts. One result can serve many sibling markets.

Needs an additional path

Full tables, exhaustive negatives, final totals and nuanced conditions. Use accepted official-source capture or review.

Run the paired pilot, build the narrow receipt/bundle architecture, and compare native verification with ZK. We have enough evidence to test this direction, not enough to promise a success percentage.
