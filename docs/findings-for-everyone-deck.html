Can an AI settle a bet from a newspaper email?

We give one AI a betting question and ask it to write a rule. We give a second AI that rule and one New York Times newsletter email and ask: does this email prove the answer? This week we swapped both AIs for a stronger model and measured what changed.

161

1,520

How it works

Two AIs, one email, no peeking

1 · the bet

A market question

"Will X happen by date Y?" with its official fine print, exactly as traders see it.

→
2 · the rule-writer

Writes a checklist

Sees only the question and fine print. Never sees the email, the outcome or the payout. Writes what an email would have to say for each side to be proven.

→
3 · the reader

Reads one email

Gets the checklist and a single complete email. No search, no memory of other emails, no hints. Must quote the exact sentence it relied on.

→
4 · the score

Did it find the fact?

We already know which fact each email contains. A pass means the right answer and an exact quote from the email.

The decoy emails are the safety check. If a reader says "proven" about an email that proves nothing, that's a false alarm, and false alarms are what would cost real money.

Plain reading

The checklists were mostly fine. The local reader was missing facts that were sitting in the email, and it was also the one raising false claims on emails that had nothing to do with the bet. A stronger reader fixed both at once, without becoming trigger-happy: zero false alarms on 237 decoys and zero claims on 115 weak or unrelated emails.

The third number comes from the 730-row comparison subset (160 of the 161 facts); the first two are complete over all 1,915 rows.

What "found the fact" does not mean

Finding the fact is not the same as being allowed to pay out

Example

A newsletter says a jury convicted someone. The reader correctly finds that fact. But the market's fine print says it settles on "official court records", and a newsletter is a news report, not a court record. The fact is found; the bet still cannot be settled from that email alone.

How often that bites

When we checked all 161 facts against each market's actual fine print, only 1 of 161 could be settled from the email under the original rules. The other 160 needed a source or a detail the email did not contain.

So what are we measuring?

Whether the AI can reliably pull the right fact out of a real newspaper email and prove it with a quote, without inventing anything. That is the necessary first step. Settling real money additionally needs evidence the market's rules accept, which is a data problem, not an AI problem.

Honest caveats

Read the numbers with these in mind

  • Emails, not articles. The evidence is NYT newsletter emails. Full article text is not available to us: the browser route is blocked by policy and the public NYT API returns headlines and abstracts only.
  • One test set. 161 facts in 143 emails from one mailbox. Good for comparing two systems side by side; not a forecast of accuracy on all markets everywhere.
  • The reader result is on a subset. 730 of the 1,915 rows: every real fact, every weak email, a quarter of the decoys and half the unrelated emails, chosen before any scoring. The full set would cost about three times more.
  • Could the AI just "remember" the news? We guard against it three ways: a pass needs an exact quote from the email, the decoy emails are fictional, and the reader made zero claims on emails unrelated to the bet. But a large model's background knowledge can never be fully ruled out.
  • Small differences are noise. The local reader gives slightly different answers when run twice (about 1 in 11). Differences smaller than that aren't meaningful. The 41% → 93% gap is far above it.
Cost and speed

What it takes to run

Rule-writer (Fable)

≈25 s and ≈$0.11 per rule

241 rules per run, ten at a time. List prices; under the subscription it counts against usage limits instead of dollars.

Local reader (Qwen)

Free, ≈2.3 h per run

1,915 emails judged on a Mac, four at a time. No data leaves the machine.

Fable reader

≈4 s and ≈$0.2 per email

Fast, but every email goes to the model provider and the run paused twice on usage limits.

The trade-off

The local reader is free and private but misses most facts. The hosted reader finds nearly all of them but costs money, needs the emails sent out, and is throttled. The next decision is which reader to run for real, or whether a stronger local model closes the gap.

What happens next

Three things, in order

1 · reader test: done

All 730 rows scored and the paired table is final. Next is deciding which reader to run for real: free and local but misses most facts, or hosted, throttled and nearly complete.

2 · let the system improve itself

A teacher AI reads every mistake and rewrites the checklist instructions; a new version is drawn and judged from scratch and only kept if it wins on held-out data.

3 · get more evidence

With a free NYT developer key we can add headlines and abstracts for millions of markets' date ranges. Full articles need an export route we are allowed to use.

Bottom line

Swapping in a stronger model took fact recovery from a third to over ninety percent of what is actually in the email, and the biggest gain came from the reader, not the rule-writer. The remaining gap to real settlement is about evidence the market rules accept, not about the AI's reading.
